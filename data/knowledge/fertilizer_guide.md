# Fertilizer & Soil Nutrient Management Guide

## Understanding NPK — The Three Pillars of Plant Nutrition

### Nitrogen (N)
- **Role**: Promotes vegetative growth, leaf development, and chlorophyll production.
- **Deficiency Signs**: Yellowing of older leaves (chlorosis), stunted growth, reduced tillering.
- **Excess Signs**: Dark green excessive foliage, delayed flowering, lodging in cereals.
- **Optimal Range**: 200–280 kg/ha available nitrogen in soil.
- **Common Sources**: Urea (46% N), Ammonium Sulphate (21% N), DAP (18% N), Calcium Ammonium Nitrate (25% N).

### Phosphorus (P)
- **Role**: Root development, flowering, fruit and seed formation, energy transfer.
- **Deficiency Signs**: Purple/reddish discoloration of leaves, poor root growth, delayed maturity.
- **Excess Signs**: Zinc and iron deficiency induced in alkaline soils.
- **Optimal Range**: 20–50 kg/ha available P2O5.
- **Common Sources**: Single Super Phosphate (16% P2O5), DAP (46% P2O5), Rock Phosphate.

### Potassium (K)
- **Role**: Water regulation, disease resistance, starch and sugar transport, fiber quality.
- **Deficiency Signs**: Leaf edge scorching (marginal necrosis), weak stalks, poor fruit quality.
- **Excess Signs**: Magnesium and calcium uptake inhibited.
- **Optimal Range**: 120–200 kg/ha available K2O.
- **Common Sources**: Muriate of Potash (60% K2O), Sulphate of Potash (50% K2O).

## Soil pH and Its Impact on Nutrient Availability

| pH Range | Classification | Nutrient Availability |
|:---|:---|:---|
| < 4.5 | Extremely Acidic | Aluminum toxicity, P locked, Mo unavailable |
| 4.5–5.5 | Strongly Acidic | Apply lime at 2–4 tonnes/ha. Manganese toxicity risk. |
| 5.5–6.5 | Slightly Acidic | Good for most crops. Optimal P availability. |
| 6.5–7.5 | Neutral | Ideal range for majority of field crops. |
| 7.5–8.5 | Alkaline | Iron and Zinc deficiency common. Apply FeSO4 and ZnSO4. |
| > 8.5 | Strongly Alkaline | Apply gypsum at 5–10 tonnes/ha. Sodic soil reclamation needed. |

## Organic vs Chemical Fertilizers

### Organic Fertilizers
- **Farmyard Manure (FYM)**: 10–15 tonnes/ha. Apply 2–4 weeks before sowing.
- **Vermicompost**: 2–5 tonnes/ha. Rich in micronutrients and growth hormones.
- **Green Manure (Dhaincha/Sunhemp)**: Incorporate at flowering. Adds 60–80 kg N/ha.
- **Neem Cake**: 200–500 kg/ha. Acts as both fertilizer and pest deterrent.
- **Panchagavya**: Liquid organic supplement. 3% spray at 15-day intervals.

### Integrated Nutrient Management (INM)
- Combine 50% RDF (Recommended Dose of Fertilizer) with organic manures.
- Apply biofertilizers: Rhizobium for legumes, Azospirillum for cereals, PSB for all crops.
- Reduces chemical fertilizer cost by 25–30% while maintaining yield.

## Micronutrient Deficiency Correction

| Nutrient | Deficiency Symptom | Correction |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Zinc (Zn)** | Interveinal chlorosis in young leaves, small leaves | ZnSO4 25 kg/ha soil + 0.5% foliar spray |
| **Iron (Fe)** | Interveinal chlorosis in youngest leaves | FeSO4 0.5% foliar spray, chelated Fe-EDTA |
| **Boron (B)** | Hollow stem in brassicas, poor fruit set | Borax 10 kg/ha, 0.2% boric acid foliar |
| **Manganese (Mn)** | Grey speck in oats, interveinal chlorosis | MnSO4 25 kg/ha soil application |
| **Copper (Cu)** | Die-back in citrus, reclamation disease | CuSO4 5 kg/ha |
